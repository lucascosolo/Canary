# D-01: Democratic backsliding, resilience and reversal literature (2016-2025)

- Date: 2026-10-20
- Agent: researcher (Sonnet, low effort), 25 tool uses

## Answer (verbatim; read 2026-10-20; Tandfonline/Annual Reviews returned 403, several PDFs unreadable, so items marked [abstract/secondary] were not read in full text)

1. Bermeo 2016, Journal of Democracy 27(1):5-19, https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/on-democratic-backsliding/ (DOI not verified). Abstract: coups and blatant election-day fraud are declining; executive aggrandizement and strategic electoral manipulation are rising. Because the changes are legitimated by institutions, they are harder to counter. The abstract does not say "incremental" or "strategic harassment"; the six-varieties list is not confirmed.

2. Lührmann and Lindberg 2019, Democratization 26(7):1095-1113, doi 10.1080/13510347.2019.1582029 [abstract/secondary]. A third wave began in 1994 and mainly hits democracies through gradual setbacks under a legal facade. The authors say diagnostic tools for such gradual declines are lacking. A Gothenburg Univ. release says elected politicians drove 33 of 47 episodes. Breakdown vs averted counts: not found in this paper (see item 5).

3. Haggard and Kaufman 2021, Cambridge Elements, doi 10.1017/9781108957809 [publisher summary only]. Three linked mechanisms: polarization, party-system shifts that give elected autocrats legislative power, and norm erosion that splits the opposition. Chapter 3 treats legislatures and the collapse of separation of powers. Courts' role: not found.

4. Laebens and Lührmann 2021, Democratization 28(5):908-928 [abstract/secondary]. Three cases halted: Benin 2007-12, Ecuador 2008-10, South Korea 2008-16. Horizontal (legislature, courts), diagonal (civil society, media) and electoral/vertical pressure all played a part. Erosion halted when institutional constraints (term limits, judicial independence) combined with triggers (downturns, corruption scandals).

5. Boese et al. 2021, Democratization, doi 10.1080/13510347.2021.1891413, full text read at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8336576/. Stages: onset resilience (avoid autocratization) and breakdown resilience (avert breakdown once underway). 96 episodes in democracies 1900-2019; 19 of 84 completed episodes averted breakdown (23%; 22% pre-1993, 23% after). Judicial constraints and past democratic stock help at both stages. GDP per capita helps only at onset. Regional democracy helps at breakdown. Coups hurt both stages. Legislative constraints were not significant for breakdown.

6. Gamboa 2022, Cambridge UP, doi 10.1017/9781009164085 [publisher/review summary]. Opposition strategy choices and goals decide whether aspiring autocrats succeed. Institutional opposition can thwart them, per Hagopian's blurb (Colombia vs Venezuela cases). Which extra-institutional tactics failed: not found.

7. Levitsky and Ziblatt 2018 (secondary). Four indicators: rejection of or weak commitment to democratic rules; denial of opponents' legitimacy; toleration or encouragement of violence; readiness to curtail opponents' civil liberties, including the media. Critique of operationalisation: not found; no sourced critique located.

8. Waldner and Lust 2018, Annu. Rev. Polit. Sci. 21:93-113, doi 10.1146/annurev-polisci-050517-114628. Abstract says explanations remain "inchoate". Secondary source: six families (agency, culture, institutions, political economy, social structure/coalitions, international actors) and no rigorous theory. Prediction wording not verified.

9. Nord, Angiolillo, Lundstedt, Wiebrecht and Lindberg 2025, "When autocratization is reversed: episodes of U-Turns since 1900," Democratization (secondary). V-Dem Policy Brief 42 (Jan 2025): 46% of autocratization processes starting in democracies 1900-2023 were reversed in U-turns. Last 30 years: 54% of breakdown cases, or 42% of episodes in democracies; most were reversed within about 5 years. A search summary gave 52% (all episodes) and 73% (last 30 years) but I could not source them.

## Caveats (verbatim)

Figures in items 2, 4, 6-9 are second-hand. Item 5 text has a 64 vs 70 country inconsistency. Verify items 3, 7, 8 and the 9 percentages against the primary text before citing.

## Orchestrator note

Two numbers matter most for calibration language and must be verified against primary text before they appear in the UI: Boese et al.'s 23% breakdown-averted share (read in full, usable) and the V-Dem U-turn share (secondary, two different figures circulating). The qualitative findings converge: judicial constraints and democratic stock are the strongest protective factors; erosion halts when institutional constraints meet a trigger; and no theory yet predicts backsliding reliably, which is an argument for an indicator-and-evidence system rather than a predictive model.
